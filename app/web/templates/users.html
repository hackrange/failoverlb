If nobody can get in at all, there is a way back from the command line on the box: docker exec nginx-fleet-manager python -m app.reset_admin admin --reset-totp

What each role can do

  • viewer can look at everything and change nothing. Good for people who need to see what is going on during an incident.
  • operator can change sites, pools and certificates and apply config. Cannot touch users or cluster trust. This is the right role for most of the people doing day to day work.
  • admin can do all of that plus manage users, join nodes, fail over, and upgrade nginx.

Changing somebody's role signs them out, so the new permissions apply straight away rather than the next time they happen to sign out.

Resetting an authenticator

Use this when somebody has lost their phone and has no recovery codes left. It takes the old authenticator off the account. It does not set a new one up, so the next time they sign in with their password they get the setup page and scan a fresh code. Their sessions all get signed out at the same time.

Be careful with it

Taking the second factor off an account is exactly what somebody would do to get into it. Make sure you actually know who is asking, over something other than email or chat, before you click it. Every reset is written to the audit log with your name on it.

The last admin

The tool will not let you disable, delete or demote the only remaining enabled admin. An empty admin list means nobody can fix anything and the way back involves a shell on the box.
